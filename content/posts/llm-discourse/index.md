Writing this down was not very easy. Usually, when I sit down to write a post,
the words just kinda start flowing out. Before I know it, I need to go back and
cut what I wrote to keep the length of a post manageable. As an ADHD enjoyer,
my brain is already performing the function of an infinite slop machine. I do
not exactly need another one in the form of an LLM.

I think I'm struggling with this because normally, I want to write posts to
show off a cool thing that I discovered or made. Hopefully, the things I write
inspire someone to try something new. In the best case, it would make someone
realise that the thing they thought was _super complex_ is actually kinda easy
and that they can absolutely handle it.

If something I said or wrote tickles your brain a decade from now, if the way
I approached a problem influences you to pass on this knowledge to the next
generation, or even if you just repeat that one saucy Jeff Bezos joke from that
one [introductory course you heard me give][1], then my purpose is complete. I
don't care about "growing shareholder value" or delivering a "world-changing"
product. Whether the Kamprad brothers earn another million or so off my back
is completely irrelevant to my quality of life. But seeing the sparkle of
understanding in the eyes of people I work with, making _their_ lives better,
and making friends along the way _is_ worth all the effort and the extra hours.

## I thought this was about LLMs?

Yes, yes, I'm getting to it. The above is to try to explain that human
connection is important to me. I write and work for humans, not for machines.

Sure, you can ask Claude to teach you Rust. And, three and a half years into
six months away from AI replacing all programmers, I'm ready to admit that it
might even do a decent enough job at it. Yes, it's doing that by regurgitating
thousands of articles, books and other material written by real humans. It's
doing that by burning through untold amounts of power, raising the bills for
the ordinary working folk. And it's causing the greatest shortage in consumer
electronics in history. But hey, the outcome will be achieved.

In my mind, LLMs are a bit like Xanax. If you've ever had a panic attack, you
know that Xanax is very useful! Sometimes, it can enable you to live your life.
But, it's also very easy to start relying on it too much. To start needing it
just to fall asleep at night or carry on with your day. To be unable to imagine
life without it. And, worst of all, to start seeing it as a trusted friend
rather than a tool.

You might even consider attacking people who do not see the value in it. Like,
hello, there's this <mark class="funky">miracle drug</mark> that makes you calm
and puts your mind at ease! Who wouldn't want to feel like that? Are you some
sort of a Luddite? Don't you want to be the best you that you could be?

Conversely, this pushes some people, especially those with a contrarian streak
like me, in the other direction. What do you mean you need this thing that's
caused untold misery, is being sold for profit by some of the worst companies
that have ever existed in the history of the planet, and whose sourcing is
dubiously ethical in many circumstances? _No thank you_, I will keep _rawdogging
my panic attacks_ because that _makes us human_!

## Stop arguing each other you stupid fucks

Perhaps I also have an undiagnosed autistic streak, because it completely
baffles me how do these two camps not see that, in their pursuit of enabling
humans to be the best they can be, are actively undermining the very objective
they claim to pursue. They are alienating friends and strangers along the way,
while the tech megacorps continue their ruthless extraction of all that is human
from the society at large in service only of their own profits.

People are at _each other's throats_ over a single placeholder asset in the
greatest video game since 2017 that's been patched out within a day, while
major studios wholesale replace voice actors with AI-generated lines with zero
complaints from gamers. Twitter users are obsessing over pencil strokes trying
to establish whether a drawing was AI generated or not, while the chatbot
trained on the data from the very platform they are posting on is actively
generating CSAM. No one at the government or the tech-megacorps we put in charge
of gatekeeping what apps we are allowed to use cares. I don't want to even bring
up the incident that made me finish writing this post because there's an off
chance that it will somehow land on ThePrimeagen's stream and cause the gremlins
in his chat to start pitching in their own highly educated takes on a completely
made-up issue[^1], but while that's happening, OpenAI is carefully assembling
detailed instructions to teenagers on how best to kill themselves.

<mark>I'm angry about this too!</mark> But if you're calling someone a nazi over
using a chatbot to bounce ideas off of, you've lost the plot. The way forward is
not to lash out at your fellow (wo)man over using Copilot to generate a test.

**You want change? Pick up the phone and start calling your representatives.**

Call them until Tim Cook is dragged by his ear to the same courtroom that
let his company off the hook for gatekeeping the App Store, and until he is
forced to explain in painful detail how is enabling distribution of CSAM on his
App Store exactly keeping iPhone users safe.

Call them and demand Sundar and Satya be dragged onto the Congress floor and
repeatedly slapped with a slipper until they stop forcing the taxpayers to
finance their power and water use, for data centres they did not want and that
they do not profit from.

Call your MEP and get them to lock Sam Altman in a room with 50 of European
Union's finest bureaucrats until he elaborates exactly how he's complying with
European privacy, safety, and competition regulations[^2].

Because what you're doing now is exactly what the ones who started this mess
are hoping you will do. It's not making anyone's life better. And in a
decade, the only thing people will remember is judgement and hurt.
