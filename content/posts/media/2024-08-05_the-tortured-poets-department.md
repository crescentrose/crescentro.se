It happened about halfway through the third night of the Amsterdam leg of the Eras tour. Taylor
Swift had just finished performing champagne problems from Evermore, and tens of thousands of
swifties, as they did after every song thus far, broke out into a thunderous applause.

However, instead of keeping the spectacular and incredibly well produced show going, Taylor Swift
simply stood there, in the middle of her trademark shapeshifting stage that somehow made the
entirety of Paramore look small and insignificant but was just about the right size for her. And the
audience kept going - cheering, yelling, whistling, shouting, clapping, louder and louder. The few
people around me still sitting in their chairs all started getting up and making more and more
noise.

This kept going for about half a minute until Taylor did something unthinkable for a professional
musician: she took out her, heretofore carefully hidden, earplugs. As she did that, she visibly
winced from the sheer magnitude of the sound generated by eighty thousand humans making as much
noise as humanly possible. Yet, she remained standing there, turning her head around, simultaneously
looking like a small child in a candy shop, and the Eye of Sauron ready to smite the first person
she sees who is not clapping. And for another half a minute or so, until she thanked the audience
and kept going with august, I believe I truly experienced what it must have been like to attend one
of Stalin's speeches.

This could have been, of course, a carefully rehearsed sequence of events that occurs every night,
and I would have been none the wiser. But I am not sure which is worse - that she would willingly
include a "everyone claps for me a lot and I get tinnitus" section in her set, or that she was truly
surprised that her audience is so enamored by her that it is willing to cheer her on for several
minutes while she does nothing but stand there and take it in.

Regardless, that was the moment in which I understood. The Tortured Poets Department is not
a victory lap of a person who has utterly and completely demolished any competition on the
charts. It is not a demonstration of ingenuity and creativity by an artist at the peak of her
career. It is not even a celebration of the immense cultural phenomenon that is Taylor Swift,
a larger-than-life icon not just appreciated but outright worshiped by many.

The Tortured Poets Department is an album that is designed to be shouted back at Taylor Swift, the
persona and the icon, by a stadium full of people. It is, perhaps inadvertently, perhaps
purposefully, a collection of affirmations - yes, I am still a victim of great injustices, yes, some
people are out to hurt me, yes, I need to keep smashing records to be valid in this world. TTPD is
an album that binds the fans together in a frenzy of "us against them" - there's Taylor Swift, only
the world's most successful pop star, a single woman that is capable of doing the job of four
British dudes. And there's The Rest - her ever expanding collection of exes who have all wronged her
in one way or another, the envious media, the competition foaming at the mouth to get the top spot
on the charts.

The Tortured Poets Department is not for you, or for me. It is for the religious ones.

But it has one or two bangers that, in better times, would have probably been shoved towards the
bottom of a better album.

★★☆ (2.5/5)
