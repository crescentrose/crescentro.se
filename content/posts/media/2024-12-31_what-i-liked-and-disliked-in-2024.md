Throughout 2024 I kept a [ranked list of music releases on my RYM
page](https://rateyourmusic.com/list/crescentrose/what-i-liked-and-disliked-in-2024/). This is the
complete list!

Some entries do not have any comments. This is probably because I didn't have a strong opinion of it
one way or another and couldn't think of anything to say. Sorry!

Also, these are all spur of the moment, unorganized thoughts that mostly reflect how much
I personally like something and not how objectively good or bad it is. If a disaster falls upon our
society and this document is the only one that remains chronicling the year 2024 in music, please do
not use it for anything else but shits and giggles.

## 49. Katy Perry — Woman's World (single)

For most of this year, I honestly believed there is not a single track that could outdo Houdini in
its pointlessness and detachment from the real world. However, it is as if Katy Perry heard all of
the criticism of Houdini, yelled out "GET DOWN MR. PRESIDENT" and dove in face first into a massive,
steaming pile of horse excrement that is Woman's World. Congratulations, Katy! In a year full of
divisions and infighting, you actually succeeded at uniting the people in one thing: everyone
fucking hates this.

## 48. Eminem — Houdini (single)

The purpose of a lead single is to get you excited about an upcoming album, and Houdini managed to
do the exact opposite to me. The Death of Slim Shady might even be listenable according to some
reviews. Unfortunately, Eminem's lyricism seems to have reached the level of your boomer uncle's
Facebook posts and the only bar lacking from Houdini to complete the transformation is to end it
with "IT'S CALLED DARK HUMOUR, LIBERALS! 🤣".

## 47. Bleachers — Bleachers

This is not Jack Antonoff's year.

## 46. Green Day — Saviors


Five tracks into this album I caught myself thinking "damn, this first track is kinda long, I wonder
when the second one is going to start". However, given that I actually managed to make it five
tracks in, this is still a marked improvement from their previous album. Not offensively bad, just
mediocre.

## 45. Beyoncé — Cowboy Carter

I'm probably missing something here, but it just sounds boring to me. Someone with more (or less)
musical sensibilities will probably enjoy it.

## 44. Dua Lipa — Radical Optimism

It was always going to be difficult to follow up Future Nostalgia. But Radical Optimism sounded like
Dua Lipa phoning it in and playing it safe instead of taking a risk that she could afford after such
a successful record and coming up with something bolder. Radical Optimism is not bad, just boring -
which probably makes it more offensive than if it was actually bad for a reason.

## 43. Cage the Elephant — Neon Pill

It's alright. I think it's time Cage the Elephant calls it a day, though. Their last couple of
records have been very hit-and-miss, they seem creatively exhausted and perhaps they would find more
happiness in other projects.

## 42. Sexcorp. — Sexo, violência, Rio & São Paulo

Not really sure I understand what the artist was going for here, and I am not sure if I like it, but
I also don't hate it? Yet I would probably not voluntarily listen to it again.

## 41. Yves — I Did

Generic music you'd hear in a random average bubble tea shop.

## 40. Taylor Swift — The Tortured Poets Department

This is not Jack Antonoff's year.

## 39. glass beach — plastic death

glass beach was always on my radar, but never quite exactly my style. Plastic Death is objectively
a good record and there are a couple of tracks on here that I really like, but overall it's just not
for me.

## 38. St. Vincent — All Born Screaming

God damn it is that one lyric so fucking tasteless.

## 37. jschlatt — A Very 1999 Christmas

I don’t even like Christmas music. And I have no idea who jschlatt is. But for a Christmas album,
it’s quite decent - perhaps one of those that will at least reduce the unhealthy frequency with
which I listen to the great TotalBiscuit’s rendition of Santa Baby as my only acceptable Christmas
song.

## 36. Zach Bryan — The Great American Bar Scene

I didn't expect to like this, and I am still not crazy about this, but it strikes a certain chord in
a man of my age that makes it difficult to pretend to be ambivalent about it.

## 35. Vampire Weekend — Only God Was Above Us

## 34. Nelly Furtado — 7

Remember that one restaurant, diner, fast food place, whatever, that you LOVED when you were
younger? Maybe your parents took you there and you have some memories attached to that place, or you
just really enjoyed the food, or some combination of both?

Then you stop going there for some reason. Maybe you grew out of it, maybe you moved, maybe you
found something else.

At a whim, you visit it a decade or two later, now in your late twenties or early
thirties. Surprisingly, that old place is still there. It has the same name, same feel to it, even
the menus look similar. You order your favorite childhood meal, ready to be overcome by a wave of
childlike happiness rushing in from the past, ready to meet you wherever you are.

Finally, your food arrives. You bite into it, but... it just doesn't hit the same. Did you grow out
of it? Was it always like this? Did the old chef retire and forget to pass the secret ingredient to
the new one? Did the management screw something up? Well, whatever it is, you finish your mediocre
meal with a side of nostalgia, take another look around and finally, leave - there's nothing left
here for you. Try as you might, you will never replicate that one childhood memory.

That's 7.

## 33. Justice — Hyperdrama

Justice proves their reputation as Charli XCX for straight men.

## 32. Halsey — The Great Impersonator

I honestly have no strong feelings about this album, and if it wasn't for all the Discourse, I doubt
I'd have paid much attention to it. Devoid of context, it's a Halsey record like most others. Within
the context, it's ... still a Halsey record (despite popular opinion, the sound waves do not in fact
change depending on your knowledge of celebrity lore). I don't see anything particularly offensive
about it (Fantano) or groundbreaking about it (Twitter), so I'll leave it there.

## 31. Sabrina Carpenter — Short n' Sweet

Espresso and Please, Please, Please were unavoidable this summer, and rightfully so. But the rest of
this album does not really go anywhere beyond that.

Addendum: Later in the year, I also found Taste enjoyable, but not sufficiently to move the album up
or down.

## 30. SOPHIE — SOPHIE

I really want to like this record. But the unfortunate thing about experimental music is that
sometimes the experiment whiffs. I appreciate and respect the approach that SOPHIE's estate took to
this record, and I am thankful that we got to hear it. I just believe that a lot of these tracks
were meant to be further experimented with and iterated upon before they got to see the light of
day.

## 29. Mannequin Pussy — I Got Heaven

## 28. Origami Angel — Feeling Not Found

I can't truly bring myself to write anything negative about Origami Angel, and I am not super sure
why that is. Perhaps I've mellowed in my old age and I am just happy that someone is carrying forth
the pop punk emo torch. Perhaps it's because Feeling Not Found sounds like what I imagine Paramore
would sound if they came to prominence in the early 2020s and also if Hayley Williams was
a dude. And writing anything negative about Paramore would feel like kicking a kitten. Even if that
kitten meows for food all the time, bites you and sometimes pukes on the carpet. You still love that
damn creature. I don't really know where I am going with this anymore. I love my cat. And this album
is decent.

## 27. SOFT PLAY — Heavy Jelly

This is as far up as I can put an album solely off one track in good conscience, but god damn does
Punk's Dead deserve it. I did not know that my life needed an ethereal, angelic Robbie Williams
rattling off comments left by haters on Instagram that then turns into some pretty banging punk
rock. The rest of the album is certainly a collection of tracks.

## 24. Geordie Greep — The New Sound

A bit too neurotic for my taste.

## 23. Little Simz — Drop 7

## 22. Megan Thee Stallion — MEGAN

In between songs about having sex, which I respect (even though, being a RYM user, I can not relate
to them as much), there are some pretty powerful themes on mental health, personal experiences, and
dealing with trauma. As much as we respect a quality masturbation anthem in this household, after
listening I was left wishing there were one or two more COBRAs and one or two fewer Downstairs DJs.

## 21. JPEGMAFIA — I Lay Down My Life For You

I've never been a huge JPEGMAFIA enjoyer, probably because he makes music that is almost definitely
not meant for me. Yet this record caught something in me that made me come back to it multiple times
to truly appreciate it. While I have not yet been converted into a fan I absolutely respect this
record and the man behind it.

## 20. Megan Thee Stallion — MEGAN: Act II

"If MEGAN was so good, why's there no MEGAN 2" mfs when I show them this record

## 19. Charli XCX — brat and it's completely different but also still brat

The idea of a remix album that's more like a set of "mirror" tracks is really cool. And while not
many tracks live up to their original counterparts, they each extend and develop the Brat Auditory
Universe in their own ways. Some remixes, like 360 and Guess, stray a bit too far into the silly
territory, but there are also stand-outs like Sympathy is a knife and So I. In fact, I would say
that the entire remix album is worth listening to just for the juxtaposition of the original So
I and the remixed version, which complement each other beautifully.

## 18. Shygirl — Club Shy

A fun collection of dance tracks that does not wear out its welcome - Shygirl was not on my radar
before this release, but I am looking forward to whatever she comes up with next.

## 17. Nala Sinephro — Endlessness

I am not super big on jazz, but Endlessness is something quite different. Maybe it’s because
a couple of tracks sound like chiptune that blends together with strings and pianos that appear as
if coming out of this world? Whatever it is, I do not regret purchasing this album. I recommend
listening to it with a cup of tea while cuddling your cat.

## 16. DJ Sabrina the Teenage DJ — Hex

DJ Sabrina the Teenage DJ is another name I've only discovered this year. And my life is better for
it. Hex is a whimsical collection of house bangers that soundtracked a significant portion of my
summer and autumn months.

## 15. DJ Sabrina the Teenage DJ — Sorcery

## 14. Allie X — Girl With No Face

Even though I preferred Cape God, Girl With No Face is a very good record and certainly earns its
place towards the top of this list. Everyone was trying to make catchy synth-pop in 2024, and Allie
X delivered ahead of most other big names.

## 13. Jamie xx — In Waves

I can't really explain this but this record sounds a little bit like two records at the same
time. There's a bit of a dissonance between the "lyrics" (or spoken parts, really) and the actual
music. Neither of them are bad, but at times it can feel like eating french fries with peanut
butter. Watching a sunset burst through rain clouds on a train ride while listening to abstract
narratives about dancers on a blue dot (not sure about this one as it's very cryptic and subtle but
I think they mean the Earth) has probably made the record more impactful than it actually is if you
put it on while working or something, but it was enjoyable nonetheless.

## 12. Reol — HIDDEN BLUES

Do not trust me here - having released No Title, one of my favourite songs of all time, Reol could
probably release a record that does nothing but repeatedly kick me in the shins for 90 minutes, and
I would likely still listen to it repeatedly. HIDDEN BLUES is, thankfully, significantly less
violent, so I'll be enjoying it without hesitation.

## 11. Bishop Briggs — Tell My Therapist I'm Fine

## 10. Poppy — Negative Spaces

It might be difficult to reconcile this release with Bubblebath, the first Poppy release I heard,
but Negative Spaces is unmistakably Poppy in the way she manages to blend about half a dozen
different genres almost seamlessly. The alt-metal direction that she's been trending towards ever
since Am I a Girl? has mostly panned out well here, and tracks like "vital" and the eponymous
"negative spaces" are prime highlights of that. The weirdly beautiful and perfectly short "tomorrow"
stood out to me on the first listen and it has become one of my favourite tracks of the year. If
there's a weak point on this album, it's definitely Poppy's desire to SCREAM VERY LOUDLY, something
that I am still not very convinced she has the vocal capability for, but thankfully that's contained
to a handful of tracks.

## 9. Kendrick Lamar — GNX

This album sounds like the victory lap of a man who had the entire planet dancing their summer away
to a track in which he calls one of the biggest stars of our generation a pedophile. And... wait,
what's this... it's... it's... by God, it's JACK ANTONOFF WITH A STEEL CHAIR!!!

## 8. Madelline — Make More Money (single)

This song is a rare example of absolute pop perfection - a fun, cheeky, self-aware, endlessly
replayable bop that gets stuck in your head for weeks to come. Even the cover is impeccable. After
Dopamine, D E a D and this track, I am one hundred percent ready for Madelline to drop at least an
EP.

## 7. Charlotte Wessels — The Obsession

After Delain ceremoniously shot themselves in the foot, I was not expecting to hear much more either
from them or their constituent parts. But Chasing Sunsets had me standing up at attention, and the
rest of the album manages to follow up. The Obsession marks a strong, confident transition into
Charlotte's solo career, and I am looking forward to once again blowing out my eardrums to
Charlotte's incredible vocal range paired with wonderful songwriting.

## 6. Porter Robinson — SMILE! :D

Dark lyrics on top of upbeat instrumentals have always been my weak spot, and Russian Roulette off
SMILE! :D is probably the most extreme example - the cognitive dissonance of a dance track
convincing you not to kill yourself is genuinely incredible. A wonderful record.

## 5. Billie Eilish — HIT ME HARD AND SOFT

Billie Eilish would like all of us to know that she loves women - and her desire to inform us of
that is so strong she wrote an entire album about it, and then hopped on a Charli XCX track a couple
of months later just to make sure we all still remember that. Thankfully, it slaps, and might even
be her best record yet.

## 4. Kendrick Lamar — Not Like Us (single)

Coughing baby vs. Hydrogen bomb

## 3. Father John Misty — Mahashmashana

I have not heard a single song by Father John Misty before this record. Father, forgive me. I knew
this album would rank highly on this list by track two, and the rest of the album just reinforced my
opinion. His sermon is equal parts dry millennial humour, faux-philosophical spiritual contemplation
that only takes itself seriously some of the time, colorful orchestral ballads, funk-ish rock-ish
bangers, and a few sarcastic quips as garnish. Thank you Father!

## 2. Charli XCX - brat

Everything that needs to be said about this record has been said already. Brat propelled Charli XCX
as close to mainstream as she ever was, and for a very good reason - each track on Brat is
a carefully crafted love letter to the early 2000s, ranging from tracks with incredible depth,
extremely revealing and sometimes even uncomfortably personal lyrics, to club bangers about doing
coke. Unfortunately, I dislike cokeheads. Hence the second place.

## 1. Magdalena Bay — Imaginal Disk

After the wonderful Mercurial World, I was not expecting such an amazing follow-up. Yet, in a year
dominated by synthpop and dance-inspired music, MagBay took everyone back to school and delivered
unto us Imaginal Disk. This album is an experience that can not easily be summed up in a couple of
sentences, so I will spare you my poor attempts at describing the indescribable and kindly request
that, if you listen to anything off this list, it be this album.
