There's this curious, pretentious way that people tend to describe albums that they claim have
impacted or even changed their lives. First, it's always something respectful like The Beatles, or
Bob Dylan, or Pink Floyd - no one is brave enough to write such a dissertation about a Limp Bizkit
album, I guess. Then, they wax poetically about the romantic way they were introduced to this
work - the cover art spoke to them at the record shop so they scrounged up their last nickel to buy
it, or they heard it on the radio while driving home after their first kiss with their future
wife. And then, they inevitably touch upon the complex, layered lyrics about the meaning of love and
life, or the intricate, revolutionary melodies that connected them, if just momentarily, to the
divinity of the great beyond. Or some bullshit like that.

No Title is none of those things. No Title does not come from an acclaimed artist at the peak of
their career. Like many others, I first ran into it on world's premiere circle clicking
simulator - not a particularly romantic place. And the music? The opening, aptly named "-Opening-",
is a grating assault on the senses that would have Skrillex rolling in his grave if he was dead and
"Gigantic O.T.N" is a song about cocks, particularly, the large ones (don't worry, dear Western
reader, if what they say about Japanese men is true I'm sure yours is big enough to qualify).

Yet, after the first, forgettable, leg of the album, you reach the interlude and the title
track. And this is... different.

When I first heard No Title the track at the tender age of 19, it sounded like an angel descended
directly from Heaven and sang straight into my ears*. I've never heard anything like it. Reol was
simultaneously singing and telling a story, barely stopping in between her sentences to catch
a breath. Her vocals were accentuated by a dreamy, almost ethereal melody that was so simple, yet so
effective. For four minutes, a person that I have never met and who I realistically should have
never even heard of, spoke to me directly from the other half of the planet, and assured me that
what I was feeling was real and valid, because here she was, singing about it. Almost like a form of
weird astral projection.

No Title has stayed with me ever since then. For the past ten years, I keep rediscovering its beauty
over and over again. Every time I come back to it, it feels like an embrace of an old friend. My
past self is dead. I'm not sure if I am the person I always dreamed of being. Nor if I ever will
be. Nothing is forever. But we keep going. Let's do this again, and again, and again. No matter
what. It will be okay, because she will sing to me.

Yeah, it's embarrassing that Reol, out of all the people in this world, rewired my brain in the same
way people claim that, say, The Dark Side Of The Moon did. It's almost too embarrassing to
admit. This admission is an instant "I win" button granted to anyone who I try to engage in an
online argument about music. Yet, in four minutes, a 21-year-old Japanese woman did what took those
old geezers of the past entire albums to do.

In the years since this album was released, Reol found fame and success, something that I am
incredibly happy for. She released music that is, objectively, much much better than anything on
this album. And I love it for her. But, you can pry No Title from my cold, dead hands.

★★★★★ (5.0/5)
