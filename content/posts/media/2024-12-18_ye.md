title = "Ye's Final Album: A Retrospective Review"
description = "My review of Kanye West's album Ye (2018)"

+++

Change is, we are told, gradual. This applies to music as well. There are very few musical acts
throughout history whose discographies contain a singular inflection point, an album that clearly
demarcates a “before” and an “after”. Yet, looking at the catalogue of the now legendary Kanye West,
there’s a very clear cut between his projects as **¥$** in the *Vultures* trilogy, *Donda 2* and the
subtly named *JESUS IS KING*, and the rest of his opus that is much more widely beloved and
celebrated, not just in the name of the artist, but also in the quality of the music and the story
behind it.

This point is, naturally, **ye** - a project that intermingles Kanye’s personas as, well, Kanye and
Ye, and that offers an unique glimpse into what could have been. On ye, Kanye West bares his soul
open, as he begins his transformation into a different person, unrecognizable from his past.

Opening with the harrowing _“I Thought About Killing You”_, we as the audience are introduced to
Kanye’s deepest, most intrusive thoughts, and the spoken word makes the track as uncomfortable to
listen to as it must have been for him to expose them to the world, knowing the judgement that he is
subjecting himself to. On the next several tracks, Kanye alternates between his many personalities,
sometimes putting himself down just as much as he brings himself up in the same verse. Kanye is
painfully aware of his failings. But, he dresses it up in just enough veneer to make it seem
apologetic and self-aware. This culminates in one of the highlights on the album, _“All Mine”_:
a track that’s simultaneously corny to the point of absurdity, but also hints at Kanye’s
introspectiveness in the infamous _“I could have Naomi Campbell / And still might want me a Stormy
Daniels”_ verse.

This is when we have to take a brief detour. As in every review of Kanye’s work, we must acknowledge
the harm that he has done in his many controversies - from the “slavery is a choice” comment, over
his appearances on white nationalist podcasts, to outright idealization of fascist ideologies, Kanye
as Ye has been a polarizing figure. However, Ye is almost unrecognizable from the reasonably sane
Kanye West. Sure, the Kanye West we see here is still an asshole, but more of the George Costanza
brand.

And in this we see the true turning point of Kanye/Ye’s career. On _“No Mistakes”_, while still
retaining the façade of pride, he acknowledges the harm he has done in his vitriol, and in the
wonderful _“Ghost Town”_, he quite literally frees himself from his past. Finally, the album wraps
up with _“Violent Crimes”_, a love letter to his daughter in which he realizes his mistakes, even
admitting that he is afraid of karma catching up for him for the ways he treated women in the past.

As we now know, Kanye West’s path forward would not be easy. He would go on to make many more
mistakes. But, along the way, he would create numerous critically acclaimed albums like _My
Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy_, _The Life of Pablo_, and, of course, the legendary _The College
Dropout_, now seen as one of the most influential of its time. While he has indeed done irreparable
harm as Ye, in his later years Kanye’s story would be that of redemption: today, even after his
unfortunate passing, he still serves as a role model for many people struggling with mental issues,
especially the easily radicalized vulnerable young men. And while his other albums might be better
or more influential, I would argue none of them were as important to his career and life as this
one.

One shudders to imagine what could have happened had this transformation not occurred. With the
topic of CERN’s latest particle accelerator coming online next week, many hypernet and quantnet
conspiracy theorists have floated the idea of some bizarro accident where random events will start
flowing in reverse, leading to the popular maymay of “Evil Kanye” who starts his career with College
Dropout and ends it in the miserable, failed Vultures trilogy. Of course, that is all just
pseudo-scientific bullshit, but even as a thought experiment, could you even imagine the sheer waste
of talent and the fall from grace that Kanye would have experienced in such a timeline, and the
damage he would have done?

Truly, if such a universe exists, I am very glad we do not have to experience it. Instead, ye stands
as a beacon of light in Kanye’s career and, even in its occasional messiness, a moment of personal
triumph, as well as a reminder what is possible when you recognize you need support, seek it out,
take the necessary medications, and put in the effort of working on yourself.

★★★★ (4.0/5)
