title = "I must have listened to this album a thousand times"
description = "My review of Metric's album Fantasies (2009)."
date = 2024-08-06
+++

This is not actually a hyperbole - looking at my Last.fm data, as of writing this review, in the
past thirteen years I've listened to tracks from this album a grand total of 1083 times.

I don't remember what I had for breakfast this morning but I can confidently recite every single
line from every single track. I've never played the guitar but Jimmy Shaw's riffs have embedded
themselves so deeply into the core of my being that I think they would simply manifest themselves in
my fingers if I ever picked up the instrument. Hell, at this point I am not sure if I enjoy
Fantasies because it's such a spectacular album, or if over the course of the past decade my brain
has been physically wired to consider it as such.

Obviously, I like this record. And I think it's for a good reason. The first seven tracks are
basically flawless. From vocals through lyricism to the instrumentation, everything about these
tracks is peak indie rock. The beating drums on Help I'm Alive are iconic. The vocals and the
overall atmosphere of Twilight Galaxy could bring a man back from the brink of death. I think the
"keep doing it wrong, keep singing along" line might have saved my life at some
point. Simultaneously haunting and exciting, energetic yet reassuring, strained but easygoing, this
run of tracks accomplishes so many things yet makes it look effortless.

Admittedly, as much as I love this record, the final three tracks can feel a bit tacked on
- especially Stadium Love, which seems thematically somewhat detached from the rest of the album. It
is my great frustration that the bonus track Waves did not make it onto the standard edition of this
album. Waves would be such an amazing closing track that it would demand another full review like
this one to do it justice. Do yourself a favor and just consider Waves a part of this album.

Nonetheless, the final three tracks are still very very good and round up Fantasies nicely. And
after all this time, I've grown to love them just as much as the rest of this wonderful record that
never fails to make me feel seen, a part of something larger, like things in this world make some
sort of sense after all.

I saw Metric live a couple of years ago as they were touring in support of Formentera. Twenty three
years this band has been playing live and they still seem at their peak. Emily Haines, a woman
almost double my age, jumped around the stage so much it made me feel sorry about my physical
state. And I'm not even that out of shape (despite writing reviews on RYM). Actually, for most of
the show, I was just too overwhelmed by feelings to even sing along. The sixteen year old me
listening to a dodgy pirated MP3 of Gimme Sympathy while waiting for a bus would have likely punched
me for that. Oh well.

I've listened to tracks from this album a grand total of 1083 times. God willing, this number will
have another digit by the time I die.

★★★★☆ (4.5/5)
