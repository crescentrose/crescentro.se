I've always been a fan of predictable cycles - something happening with some regularity is
a reassuring way to know that the world is still the same and that there are still things we can
count on. For example, we can, with absolute certainty, predict that about once a year there will be
a total solar eclipse. The economy will go down a bit after it goes up a lot. Every September, Apple
will introduce a new iPhone. Every couple of years, Spotify will take more money from the users and
give less to the artists. Taylor Swift will inevitably break a sales record. Weird Al will come up
with a polka-themed interpretation of the biggest hits of the past few years. And, of course, Eminem
will crawl out of the woodwork to mockingly rant about random stuff that bothers him for a couple of
minutes.

In the first several iterations of this cycle, Eminem's rants were usually witty, and at least
somewhat funny and culturally relevant. The Real Slim Shady is a legitimate banger that establishes
Eminem as our anti-hero, names a few relevant names, gives the world's dumbest argument in favour of
gay marriage, and exits the stage with some much needed self-praise. With Without Me, this formula
has been perfected - Eminem starts off with a verse intended to shock you, then goes on to stroke
his ego and diss a few of his contemporaries, and finishes off by congratulating himself on being
the champion of free speech and the only one telling it like it is. By We Made You, this formula was
getting a little bit stale, but we still had some laughs. And of course, the cycle continued with
minor alterations to this formula through Rap God and The Ringer.

That the few earlier tracks aged relatively well is probably not a controversial opinion. You can go
back to The Real Slim Shady or Without Me and still find it, if not culturally relevant anymore, at
least an entertaining snapshot of Eminem's view of the world. After all, firing shots at the FCC,
the Bush administration, Kim Kardashian, Limp Bizkit and the Grammies is entertaining! And really,
why does Will Smith think he is better than us just because he doesn't use the word "fuck" in his
songs? Eminem might not have been the voice of the generation, but at least you could listen to the
tracks and find a couple of good points.

Even some of the later iterations of this cycle had their moments. Rap God is a fun track because of
its novelty, and when you turn on the subtitles, you can hear Eminem putting himself down as much as
he build himself up. The Ringer, for all its faults as a track that's more fun to read the Genius
page about than to listen to, can at least be taken as "what if 'old man yells at cloud' was
a song". They might not have had so much staying power and long-term relevance as the previous
tracks, but at least you could listen to them and think to yourself, yeah, this guy Eminem, we might
not really see eye to eye on some topics, but you know, there's something to what he's saying. Lil
Pump really does sound like a Lil Wayne clone, and who likes MGK anyway? Look, he's even saying he's
"not dissing" mumble rappers, just that it's not for him! No matter how bad things get, at least you
can count on Eminem to take down some of the popular mainstream artists down a notch!

And so, on Houdini, Eminem has a problem with eight year old children, and transgender cats.
